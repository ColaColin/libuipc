# Perf round 6 — a workload nobody has profiled: a tumbler full of garments

Rounds 3–5 tuned the four official benchmarks (`rigid-wrecking-balls`, `cube-wall-cloth`,
`stiff-gipc-case2`, `mas-bunny`) and the RTX 5090 acceptance A/B closed round 5 at −6.6 to −30.1 %.
Round 6 changes the *workload* instead of the hardware: a **rotating tumbler with garments inside**
— dense multi-layer cloth self-contact, continuous frictional sliding, and one kinematic drum. None
of the official four contain that mix, and PERF_METHOD §4's runner-up finding is that the largest
wins have come from profiling a workload nobody had measured.

- **Base**: tag `perf-round6-base` = `60af65e6` (round-5 head + the build-script commits).
- **Brief**: `/workspace/output/round6/AGENT_BRIEF.md`.
- **Box**: local RTX 2070 SUPER only (cc 7.5, 8 GB, FP64 1/32). **Serial**: one worker at a time,
  one GPU, because measurements contend.
- **Branches**: `perf/round6-sNN-<area>`, one step per branch cut from head at spawn, merged by the
  coordinator, step numbers assigned centrally at merge.
- **No acceptance box.** Every number here is cc 7.5. Each accepted step records a *transfer
  prediction* (PERF_METHOD §6 categories) that a future rented run can falsify — the 2070S
  over-rewards spill/register work and occupancy tuning, and that is stated per step, not at the end.

## s00 — the instrument

Scene: `libuipc-samples/examples/95_tumbler_garments/` (the sibling `94_` number was
already taken by `94_robot_hand_grasp_apple`). Evidence:
`agent_docs/performance/data/2026-09-13-round6-s00/`.

**What it is.** A horizontal-axis drum (R = 0.30 m, bore 0.40 m, three welded radial
lifters, 1644 triangles) turned at 40 rpm, with four procedurally built garments inside
— towel, pillowcase, shorts, washcloth — 18,276 triangles and 27,158 bending hinges of
`StrainLimitingBaraffWitkinShell` + `DiscreteShellBending`, self-contact and friction on.
Everything is numpy-generated; no new binary assets. 180 frames at dt = 1/60 = two full
drum revolutions.

**Drum drive.** `SoftTransformConstraint` fed the *absolute* aim transform `R_z(omega·t)`
each frame. `RotatingMotor` was rejected: it prescribes only the per-frame rotation
*increment* relative to the body's current pose (so lag becomes unmeasurable permanent
drift) and it sets the translational strength to zero (so a loaded drum falls out of
place). Measured tracking error over 720° commanded: **0.0396° max, −0.00032° final**.

**Size.** 27–30 s per 180-frame run, peak 2.0 GB of the 8 GB card. `size_sweep.md` has
the element-size, membrane-stiffness and drum-density sweeps that fixed those values.
Two of them matter physically, not just for speed:
- the cloth-machine *legacy* moduli (`E_stretch` = 1e4) crumple triangles to a third of
  their rest area, pushing the dynamic minimum triangle height under `2r + d_hat`
  (permanent self-contact). `k_stretch = E·2r = 1000 N/m` fixes that **and is 12 % faster**;
- the drum is kinematic, so its density is a conditioning knob. 2000 kg/m³ makes the
  relative PCG criterion the drum's: **+28 % PCG iterations and +20 % wall time** over
  500 kg/m³ for the same trajectory and the same 0.04° tracking error.

**Soundness** (`--verify`, full 180-frame run — `verify_run_180f.txt`):

| check | number |
|---|---|
| all positions finite | yes |
| max radius / bore limit | 0.29864 m / 0.30227 m — contained |
| max \|z\| / limit | 0.19859 m / 0.20227 m — contained |
| closest approach into a lifter box | −1.11 mm (never enters one) |
| triangle area ratio vs rest, min/max | 0.838 / 1.253 — no collapse, no inversion |
| min triangle height over the run | 3.85 mm, above `2r + d_hat` = 3.27 mm |
| max vertex speed | 3.20 m/s (bore wall speed 1.26 m/s) |
| drum tracking error | 0.0396° max over 719.9997° of 720.0° commanded |
| mean per-frame vertex displacement | 15.4 mm overall, **17.4 mm in the last quarter** — it does not settle |
| garment centroid angular travel | 557° / 714° / 332° / 739° against 720° of drum — carried and slipping |
| Newton per frame | 3–16, mean 7.87; first quarter 7.2 → last quarter 8.4 (no divergence) |
| PCG per frame | 15–705, mean 261; 207 → 276 |
| frames that failed to converge | 0 |

**The scene is not deterministic.** Frames 1–3 are bit-reproducible; PCG counts first
differ at frame 4 and Newton counts at frame 6 — i.e. as soon as the dense contact set
forms. This is *not* new to the tumbler: `cube-wall-cloth` is also non-deterministic
(Newton 508/514/520 over three runs) — but its wall scatter is 0.80 %, six times tighter.
Only `mas-bunny` (Newton 465 every run) is the sub-1 % instrument.

**Consequences for every later step that wants to gate on this scene**
- `meanFrameMs` at n=5 resolves ~9 %. Do not claim a 2–3 % win from it. n/arm for a
  2 % effect is **101**; for 5 % it is 17; for 10 % it is 5.
- **`ms per Newton iteration` is the tightest end-to-end statistic here (cv 2.05 %)**,
  because most of the wall scatter is trajectory divergence rather than throughput —
  but it is only legitimate when the change does not move Newton counts (PERF_METHOD §2.4).
  Report both, and report the counts.
- `medianFrameMs` (cv 3.13 %) is a better default than `meanFrameMs` on this scene.
- For sub-2 % work, keep using `mas-bunny` / `cube-wall-cloth` as the precision
  instrument and use `tumbler-garments` to show the win is not scene-specific.

### What this workload stresses that the official four do not

1. **Continuous collision detection over a dense self-contacting cloth pile.** The
   `InfoStacklessBVH*` family is the single largest cost centre at **30.9 %**, in absolute
   terms **7.3× `cube-wall-cloth`'s** whole BVH bill. Per launch, `filter_toi_k4` costs
   1426 µs here against 461 µs (cwc) and 300 µs (rwb) — it is *more work per launch*, not
   just more launches. Nothing in rounds 3–5 profiled a scene where CCD, not assembly,
   led the ranking.
2. **Shell bending as a first-order cost.** 18.4 % and the most expensive single kernel
   launch anywhere in the suite (3.48 ms). Normalised: **128 ns per hinge** here
   (27,158 hinges) and **145 ns per hinge** in `cube-wall-cloth` (12,160 hinges) — so the
   kernel is *intrinsically* slow per hinge and the tumbler merely has 2.2× more hinges
   and 3× more Newton iterations. A win here is measurable on `cube-wall-cloth`, whose
   scatter is 0.80 %.
3. **Contact assembly is relatively less dominant** (17.3 % vs 37.2 % in
   `rigid-wrecking-balls`), so the three rounds of `do_assemble_kernel` tuning have less
   left to give on the workload we actually care about.
4. **A very long, chaotic PCG tail.** 47,353 PCG iterations per run over 1,460 Newton
   iterations (32 per Newton) with a 14 % run-to-run spread, against `cube-wall-cloth`'s
   ~20,000 over ~510 (39 per Newton) at 1.5 %. The MAS preconditioner's inner kernels run
   52,294 times per run.

## Instrument validation (coordinator, after s00) — a null A/B measured a 4.94 % "win"

PERF_METHOD §2.7 says validate the instrument, not just the result. Before assigning any
optimization step, the coordinator ran the round's shared A/B harness
(`/workspace/output/round6/ab.py`) with **both arms set to the same env value on the same build**.
Anything it reports is by construction false.

| null A/B | ordering | meanFrameMs | medianFrameMs | ms/Newton-it | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tumbler-garments`, n=5 | ABAB, no warm-up | **−4.94 %** (p=0.23) | **−7.26 %** | **−5.52 %** (p=0.22) | false effect, arm A got the cold first run (174.9 ms against its own 147–163 ms) |
| `tumbler-garments`, n=5 | **ABBA + 1 discarded warm-up** | **−1.02 %** (p=0.82) | −4.28 % | **−1.31 %** (p=0.75) | the artefact is gone; what is left is the scene's own scatter |
| `cube-wall-cloth`, n=5 | ABBA + warm-up | +1.50 % (p=0.17) | +0.56 % | **+0.54 %** (p=0.19) | the precision instrument of this round |

Three things follow, and they govern every later step:

1. **A cold first run is worth ~5 % on this scene, and under ABAB ordering it lands entirely in the
   first arm.** The harness now runs one **discarded warm-up per arm** and orders the reps
   **ABBA**, so a monotone drift cancels instead of accumulating. Both are defaults; a step that
   overrides them must say so.
2. **The tumbler cannot gate a small effect.** Its own null moves ±1 % with single runs spanning
   134–169 ms. Treat it as the *ranking and confirmation* instrument: claim an end-to-end tumbler
   win only above ~4 %, always alongside the per-kernel scope gate. Sub-2 % work is gated on
   `cube-wall-cloth` (null ±0.54 % on ms/Newton-it) or `mas-bunny` (deterministic, Newton 465).
3. **Correction to s00's guidance: prefer the mean, not the median, on the tumbler.** s00 recommended
   `medianFrameMs` as the tighter statistic; under a null it is the *loosest* of the three
   (−7.26 % and −4.28 % against the mean's −4.94 % and −1.02 %). `ms_per_newton` is tightest, and
   only while Newton counts hold still.

The statistics themselves came through the test: every null was reported as overlapping with
p ≥ 0.17, and the iteration-count guard fired on the runs where PCG had moved. No number in this
round is trusted because the harness printed it — it is trusted when the scope gate and the
end-to-end gate agree, on a scene whose null envelope is smaller than the claimed effect.

## s01 — `__launch_bounds__` on the hinge G/H kernel: a wave-quantisation effect that flips sign between two scenes on the same GPU

Evidence: `agent_docs/performance/data/2026-09-13-round6-s01/`
(`kernel_scope.md`, `static_resources.txt`, `ptx_identity.txt`, `gate.txt`, `gate_occ1.txt`,
`nsys/`, `ab/`).

**The premise checked first (PERF_METHOD §2.10/§2.11).** Round 5's s30 won 21 % on the sibling
`StableNeoHookean3D` G/H kernel with `__launch_bounds__(128, 3)`, and nothing in rounds 3–5 ever
put a bound on the hinge kernel, which is the more extreme case: 255 registers, 3 104 B of stack
frame, 1 168 B of spill traffic, **8 of the SM's 32 warp slots**. Round 4 had already written that
this kernel "is *not* at an FP64 roof". The hypothesis was therefore the §4 pattern — a lever built
in an earlier round that was never wired into the kernel it fits best. It is wrong, and the way it
is wrong is the finding.

**The old path is provably untouched.** A first attempt moved the body into a shared
`__device__ __forceinline__` function (s30's shape). Under `-rdc=true` that is **not** neutral: it
moved the unbounded kernel from 3 104 B of frame / 392 B cmem[2] to 3 616 / 440 — i.e. it would have
made arm A of its own A/B slower than the tree it was being compared against. The shipped form puts
the parameter list and the body in macros instead, so every wrapper expands to the same token stream
and all six pre-existing instantiations keep main's exact figures (6688 / 3472 / 6976 / 3840 / 3104 /
3328 B, same cmem). **Check this class of thing on the binary, not in the source.**

**What the bound actually does.** sm_75: 40 SMs, 65 536 registers and 32 warp slots each. 255
registers/thread admits 8 warps per SM *whatever the block size*, so the s20/s24 launch ramp — which
moves warps between blocks — cannot change occupancy at all. 168 registers admits 12.

| scene | grid x block | blocks/SM at 255 reg | rounds | blocks/SM at 168 | rounds | measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cube-wall-cloth | 380 x 32 | 8 (320 resident) | **2** | 12 (480 resident) | **1** | **1.7555 -> 1.6656 ms/launch, −5.12 %** |
| tumbler-garments | 425 x 64 | 4 (160 resident) | **3** | 6 (240 resident) | **2** | **3.4488 -> 3.8728 ms/launch, +12.29 %** |

cube-wall buys a whole round and wins 5 %. The tumbler's rounds x warps product is unchanged
(3x8 = 2x12), so no round is saved and the bound delivers nothing but **+4.8 KB per thread of extra
spill traffic** (1 168/1 168 -> 3 472/3 680 B), which costs 12.3 %. Both distributions are disjoint
(5+4 runs on cube-wall, 3+3 on the tumbler, full runs, identical launch geometry in every arm).
`(128, 4)` — 128 registers, 16 warps — is worse on both: +2.1 % and +43.9 %.

**Diagnosis, stated so a later step does not retry it.** +50 % resident warps made the kernel
**12.3 % slower** on a scene where no round was saved. This kernel is **not latency-bound**; s30's
lever does not apply to it. Its local-memory traffic is what is expensive — +4.8 KB/thread of spill
costs roughly +70 % of per-warp time. PERF_METHOD §6 says occupancy tuning "often does not transfer
— it can flip sign"; here it flips sign **between two scenes on one GPU**, which is a stronger
statement than the round-5 cross-architecture one.

**End-to-end, for the record.** Both gates are below their scenes' resolution and the harness said
so rather than being talked into a number. cube-wall-cloth n=5: `ms_per_newton` −1.42 % (t=2.95,
p=0.019) — but PCG moved −1.77 % against a −1.77 % wall change and `ms_per_pcg` is +0.01 %, so the
iteration-count guard fired and the wall number is a trajectory shift, not throughput (the scope
gate predicts −0.6 %: the kernel is 12.5 % of cube-wall GPU time). tumbler-garments n=5: +3.89 %
mean, +5.30 % per Newton, overlapping (p=0.39 / 0.17), guard fired again — the scene cannot resolve
the predicted +2 %, but both statistics carry the scope gate's sign. **The scope gate is the verdict
here; the end-to-end gates only fail to contradict it.**

**Numerics.** The bounded wrappers are bit-identical to the unbounded one. Proof
(`ptx_identity.txt`, not an argument): the TU's PTX contains all three entry points; after erasing
the mangled name, the `.maxntid`/`.minnctapersm` directives (which *are* `__launch_bounds__`), the
per-function basic-block and local-depot numbering, the bodies differ in exactly two integer
instructions — `add.s32 %r,3` strength-reduced to `or.b32 %r,3`, and two independent `setp.gt.s32`
commuted into a commutative `and.pred`. The **FP64 instruction stream is identical, all 5 144
instructions in the same order**, so contraction and rounding are fixed before ptxas runs; ptxas then
only reallocates registers. Cross-check: `uipc_test_backend_cuda` and `uipc_test_sim_case` pass with
identical assertion counts under `UIPC_DSB_OCC=1`.

### What the step measured that is worth more than the step

Stage stubbing (an uncommitted `Proj = 3` probe that writes the **unprojected** Hessian) and an
arithmetic bracket at constant occupancy, both on the shipped build:

| measurement | cube-wall-cloth | tumbler-garments |
|---|---|---|
| **PSD projection share of the kernel** | **80.5 %** | **83.3 %** |
| kernel share of scene GPU kernel time | 12.5 % | 16.5 % |
| Eigen `SelfAdjointEigenSolver` instead of s19's QL (`UIPC_MAKE_SPD_JACOBI=0`) | +19.4 % | — |
| K7 dense 12x9 basis instead of K16 blocked (`UIPC_DSB_BLOCKED_PROJ=0`) | +11.6 % | — |
| dense 12x12 eigen-solve instead of the 9x9 (`UIPC_DSB_REDUCED_SPD=0`) | **+81.0 %** | — |

So after s14, s19 and s24 the 9x9 PSD projection is **still >80 % of the most expensive kernel
launch in the suite** — the same share round 4 measured before s19, because both rounds cut the
projection's absolute cost without changing its dominance. **Everything else in this kernel — the
dihedral angle, both symbolic derivatives, the 12x12 Hessian assembly, the doublet/triplet writes —
is under 20 %.** The next step on this kernel must attack the projection or it cannot win more than
a fifth of it.

## s02 — the hinge PSD projection did not need to exist: a Gauss-Newton bending Hessian

Evidence: `agent_docs/performance/data/2026-09-13-round6-s02/`
(`static_resources.txt`, `sass_identity.txt`, `gradient_bit_identity.txt`,
`gn_math_probe.{cpp,txt}`, `kernel_scope.txt`, `launch_geometry.txt`,
`scene_executes_kernel.txt`, `convergence.txt`, `verify_audit_ab.txt`,
`gate.txt`, `gate_gn0.txt`, `ab/`, `nsys/`).

**The premise, checked against the code first (PERF_METHOD §2.11).** s01 proposed
this and it survives: `sym/discrete_shell_bending.inl` really does generate
`E = L0*kappa*(theta-theta_bar)^2/h_bar` and `ddEddtheta = 2*L0*kappa/h_bar` —
a constant that does **not** depend on `theta`, so `theta_bar != 0` changes
nothing. It is non-negative for every reachable input: `L0` is a rest edge
length, `h_bar = A/(3*L0)` a rest height, and `kappa` a bending stiffness
(`E*h^3/(12(1-nu^2))`). `discrete_shell_bending_function.h::ddEddx` assembles
exactly `dthetadx * ddEddtheta * dthetadx^T + dEdtheta * ddthetaddx`, so the
first term is a PSD rank-1 outer product of `grad(theta)` (**not** of the energy
gradient — `grad(E) = E' * grad(theta)`, so the brief's `g g^T` is the dihedral
gradient, not `dEdx`) and all of the indefiniteness is in `E' * hess(theta)`.

Measured confirmation over 200 000 randomised hinges against the real
`UIPC_GENERIC` functions (`gn_math_probe.cpp`, three deformation amplitudes):

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `max abs((H_full − H_gn) − E'*hess(theta)) / max abs(H_full)` | **3.8e-16** — the split is exactly what the premise says |
| `max abs(H_gn − H_gn^T)` | **0** — exactly symmetric (the assembler writes the four diagonal 3x3 blocks in full, so this matters) |
| `min eig(H_gn) / max abs(eig(H_gn))` | **−7.1e-16**, 0 / 200 000 samples with a meaningfully negative eigenvalue |
| rank of the **PSD-projected exact** Hessian | **4.00** of 9, every sample |
| `norm(H_full − H_gn)/norm(H_full)` | 0.059 at `mean abs(theta−theta_bar)` = 0.08 rad, 0.242 at 0.38 rad, 0.672 at 1.59 rad |

The rank line is the one worth keeping: **the exact path is not "the true
Hessian" either** — its PSD projection throws away five of nine directions at
every hinge. Gauss-Newton keeps one. The global system is still positive
definite through mass, membrane and contact.

**What shipped.** `Proj = 3` in the existing
`DiscreteShellBending_do_compute_gradient_hessian_kernel` template (a template
parameter, not a runtime flag, so neither instantiation pays for the other), and
`ddEddx_gauss_newton` beside `ddEddx`. `Vdt2` is folded into the rank-1
coefficient instead of scaling the assembled matrix (12 + 78 multiplies instead
of 12 + 144 + 144), and the fill is an explicit mirrored loop so the result is
*exactly* symmetric. `UIPC_DSB_GAUSS_NEWTON=0` restores the exact Hessian
followed by the s14/s19 projection, after which `UIPC_DSB_REDUCED_SPD`,
`UIPC_DSB_BLOCKED_PROJ` and `UIPC_MAKE_SPD_JACOBI` select it as before.
The Gauss-Newton path never calls `dihedral_angle_hessian` at all — that is why
the win is larger than s01's 80.5 % projection share.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Proj1 Solver1` (shipped exact) | 255 | 3 104 | 10 664 | 4 350 | 1 730 |
| **`Proj3 Solver1` (Gauss-Newton)** | **208** | **1 248** | **2 464** | **379** | **355** |

**The old arm reproduces main's binary** (the round-6 lesson from s01). sha1 of
the SASS instruction stream, main `1bc7badd` vs this branch: the shipped
`Proj1 Solver1`, the energy kernel and five of the six other instantiations are
**identical**. One cold rollback instantiation (`Proj1 Solver0`) jitters —
same instruction count, all 5 441 FP64 opcodes in the same order, 136 lines
differing only in BRA/BSSY targets and LDL/STL widths — which is whole-TU
register-allocation noise (on the previous build of the same source it was
`Proj0 Solver0` that moved instead). It is not an arm of any measurement.

**Numerics: not bit-identical, and not claimed to be.** Same energy, same
gradient, different Hessian.
- *Energy*: a different kernel, untouched, SASS hash identical to main.
- *Gradient*: **bit-identical, proved on the binaries**. `UIPC_DSB_GN_VERIFY=1`
  (shipped, zero cost when off) runs the real `<Proj=1,Solver=1>` kernel,
  snapshots the gradient doublets, runs `<Proj=3,Solver=1>` over the same inputs
  and counts mismatching 32-bit words on device (the repo's own s24
  `SpreadVerifier`). **116 612 804 words compared across cube-wall-cloth,
  tumbler-garments, stiff-gipc-case2 and all 95 `sim_case` cases: 0 mismatching.**
- *Converged observables*, against the exact arm's own run-to-run envelope
  (n=5/arm): cube-wall `cloth_min_y` 0.28603 [0.28094, 0.29298] -> 0.28539
  [0.27741, 0.29236]; tumbler `cloth_max_radius` 0.29798 [0.29764, 0.29851] ->
  0.29814 [0.29807, 0.29824]. Inside the envelope on both.

**Convergence robustness.** Every frame of every run of this step — 5 scenes x
5 runs x 2 arms, 3 750 frames per arm — reports `converged`, and **zero**
`hit_newton_limit` and **zero** `hit_line_search_limit` (`convergence.txt`).
The tumbler's `--verify` audit passes on every run (`verify_ok: true`), run
3x per arm on the same build (`verify_audit_ab.txt`): containment radial and
axial, no lifter penetration, no inversion, min triangle height 3.65-3.80 mm
against the 3.27 mm `2r + d_hat` floor, 0 non-converged frames, final drum
tracking error ~1e-4 deg of 720 deg commanded.

Two audit statistics have a **wider spread** in the Gauss-Newton arm and are
flagged for the validation pass rather than explained away:
`verify_area_ratio_max` 1.240 [1.193, 1.279] -> 1.288 [1.228, 1.360] over three
matched runs (a fourth Gauss-Newton run reached 1.519), and
`verify_drum_track_err_deg_max` 0.046 [0.040, 0.059] -> 1.206 [0.040, 2.151] —

drum, with the *final* tracking error unchanged. Neither trips an audit
threshold. Peak process GPU memory falls 1 793 -> 1 279 MiB, consistent with the
1 856 B/thread smaller stack frame.

runs, 3 per arm). The launch geometry is **identical in both arms** —

The tumbler's scene total falls further than the kernel's own 17.3 % share

essentially unchanged launch counts. That is a **trajectory effect** — the
Gauss-Newton step lands in slightly different configurations and the narrow
phase does less work there — not throughput, and it is reported as such. On
cube-wall-cloth the scene total moves by almost exactly the kernel's own share.

**Iteration counts are the primary result here and they are flat.** The largest
count movement anywhere is 1.87 % against an 18.59 % wall change; the harness's
guard fires at half the wall change and did not fire on any scene. The counts
move in *both* directions and overlap, so the honest statement is that the
Gauss-Newton search direction costs nothing in Newton, line-search or PCG work
on any of the five scenes — not that it converges faster.

**Control validity, checked rather than assumed (PERF_METHOD §2.2).**
`UIPC_DSB_GN_VERIFY=1` prints a comparison line only if the hinge kernel
launched. Over 10 frames each: cube-wall-cloth 9.2 M words, tumbler-garments
66.9 M, **stiff-gipc-case2 10.6 M**, mas-bunny **nothing**, rigid-wrecking-balls
**nothing**. So the round record's "cube-wall + tumbler" pair of bending scenes
is really a **trio** — `stiff-gipc-case2` exercises `DiscreteShellBending` too,
at 250 frames with a cv near 0.3 % on `ms_per_newton`, and it is the tightest
bending instrument on this box. The two ABD/FEM scenes are genuine
no-op controls.

**Transfer prediction: algorithmic — it transfers.** The change deletes work
rather than re-tuning the machine: no 9x9 eigen-decomposition, no
`dihedral_angle_hessian`, no 12x12 assembly. Static, on sm_75: −76.9 % of the
kernel's SASS instructions, −91.3 % of its FP64, −79.5 % of its local-memory
traffic, −59.8 % of its stack frame, at an unchanged launch geometry and a
nearly unchanged occupancy (8 -> 9 warps/SM). None of the categories that do not
transfer are involved — no `__launch_bounds__`, no block-size retune, no wave
quantisation. What will **not** carry over unchanged is the *scene-level*
percentage: it is the kernel's share of the scene that sets it, and PERF_METHOD
§6 records that the same kernel's share moves several points between
architectures. A 1/32-rate FP64 part like this one over-rewards the FP64 and
spill components specifically, so the −90 % per launch is an upper estimate for
a part with better FP64 throughput; the instruction-count reduction itself is
architecture-independent.

### Found outside this step's area (reported, not fixed)

**From s02:**

- **`stiff-gipc-case2` is a third shell-bending scene and nobody had noticed.** With
  `UIPC_DSB_GN_VERIFY=1` (which prints only if the hinge kernel actually launched) it compares
  10.6 M output words over 10 frames, i.e. the kernel runs there. It is 250 frames, its
  `ms_per_newton` cv is ~0.3 % over 5 runs, and its Newton count moved 0.24 % across an arm change
  that was worth −6.65 % of wall — **it is the tightest bending instrument on this box**, tighter
  than `cube-wall-cloth`. The round record's bending pair is really a trio. `mas-bunny` and
  `rigid-wrecking-balls` were re-checked the same way and genuinely launch it **zero** times.
- **The PSD projection the s14/s19 chain was optimising keeps rank 4 of 9.** Measured over 200 000
  randomised hinges: the exact 12x12 hinge Hessian is indefinite in 100 % of samples and its PSD
  projection retains exactly four positive directions every time. Three rounds of work made a rank-4
  approximation cheaper; a rank-1 one was free. Worth checking whether the *other* `make_spd` call
  sites (contact, ortho potential, membrane) are similarly close to a closed form.
- **An extra kernel in a `-rdc=true` translation unit re-rolls ptxas's register allocation for one
  unrelated instantiation in that TU.** Two builds of identical source moved a different cold
  instantiation each time (`Proj0 Solver0`, then `Proj1 Solver0`), always with the FP64 stream
  unchanged and only BRA/BSSY targets and LDL/STL widths differing. s01's lesson ("diff the binary
  before you measure") therefore needs a corollary: **diff the instantiation your A/B actually uses,
  and expect cold siblings to jitter.**
- **Peak process GPU memory on the tumbler fell 1 793 -> 1 279 MiB** purely from the smaller stack
  frame (3 104 -> 1 248 B/thread at 27 200 resident threads). Local-memory backing store is a
  first-order VRAM consumer for these 255-register kernels, which matters on the 8 GB card.
