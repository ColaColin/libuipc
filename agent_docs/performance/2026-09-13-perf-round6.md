# Perf round 6 — a workload nobody has profiled: a tumbler full of garments

Rounds 3–5 tuned the four official benchmarks (`rigid-wrecking-balls`, `cube-wall-cloth`,
`stiff-gipc-case2`, `mas-bunny`) and the RTX 5090 acceptance A/B closed round 5 at −6.6 to −30.1 %.
Round 6 changes the *workload* instead of the hardware: a **rotating tumbler with garments inside**
— dense multi-layer cloth self-contact, continuous frictional sliding, and one kinematic drum. None
of the official four contain that mix, and PERF_METHOD §4's runner-up finding is that the largest
wins have come from profiling a workload nobody had measured.

- **Base**: tag `perf-round6-base` = `60af65e6` (round-5 head + the build-script commits).
- **Brief**: `/workspace/output/round6/AGENT_BRIEF.md`.
- **Box**: local RTX 2070 SUPER only (cc 7.5, 8 GB, FP64 1/32). **Serial**: one worker at a time,
  one GPU, because measurements contend.
- **Branches**: `perf/round6-sNN-<area>`, one step per branch cut from head at spawn, merged by the
  coordinator, step numbers assigned centrally at merge.
- **No acceptance box.** Every number here is cc 7.5. Each accepted step records a *transfer
  prediction* (PERF_METHOD §6 categories) that a future rented run can falsify — the 2070S
  over-rewards spill/register work and occupancy tuning, and that is stated per step, not at the end.

## s00 — the instrument

Scene: `libuipc-samples/examples/95_tumbler_garments/` (the sibling `94_` number was
already taken by `94_robot_hand_grasp_apple`). Evidence:
`agent_docs/performance/data/2026-09-13-round6-s00/`.

**What it is.** A horizontal-axis drum (R = 0.30 m, bore 0.40 m, three welded radial
lifters, 1644 triangles) turned at 40 rpm, with four procedurally built garments inside
— towel, pillowcase, shorts, washcloth — 18,276 triangles and 27,158 bending hinges of
`StrainLimitingBaraffWitkinShell` + `DiscreteShellBending`, self-contact and friction on.
Everything is numpy-generated; no new binary assets. 180 frames at dt = 1/60 = two full
drum revolutions.

**Drum drive.** `SoftTransformConstraint` fed the *absolute* aim transform `R_z(omega·t)`
each frame. `RotatingMotor` was rejected: it prescribes only the per-frame rotation
*increment* relative to the body's current pose (so lag becomes unmeasurable permanent
drift) and it sets the translational strength to zero (so a loaded drum falls out of
place). Measured tracking error over 720° commanded: **0.0396° max, −0.00032° final**.

**Size.** 27–30 s per 180-frame run, peak 2.0 GB of the 8 GB card. `size_sweep.md` has
the element-size, membrane-stiffness and drum-density sweeps that fixed those values.
Two of them matter physically, not just for speed:
- the cloth-machine *legacy* moduli (`E_stretch` = 1e4) crumple triangles to a third of
  their rest area, pushing the dynamic minimum triangle height under `2r + d_hat`
  (permanent self-contact). `k_stretch = E·2r = 1000 N/m` fixes that **and is 12 % faster**;
- the drum is kinematic, so its density is a conditioning knob. 2000 kg/m³ makes the
  relative PCG criterion the drum's: **+28 % PCG iterations and +20 % wall time** over
  500 kg/m³ for the same trajectory and the same 0.04° tracking error.

**Soundness** (`--verify`, full 180-frame run — `verify_run_180f.txt`):

**The scene is not deterministic.** Frames 1–3 are bit-reproducible; PCG counts first
differ at frame 4 and Newton counts at frame 6 — i.e. as soon as the dense contact set
forms. This is *not* new to the tumbler: `cube-wall-cloth` is also non-deterministic
(Newton 508/514/520 over three runs) — but its wall scatter is 0.80 %, six times tighter.
Only `mas-bunny` (Newton 465 every run) is the sub-1 % instrument.

**Consequences for every later step that wants to gate on this scene**
- `meanFrameMs` at n=5 resolves ~9 %. Do not claim a 2–3 % win from it. n/arm for a
  2 % effect is **101**; for 5 % it is 17; for 10 % it is 5.
- **`ms per Newton iteration` is the tightest end-to-end statistic here (cv 2.05 %)**,
  because most of the wall scatter is trajectory divergence rather than throughput —
  but it is only legitimate when the change does not move Newton counts (PERF_METHOD §2.4).
  Report both, and report the counts.
- `medianFrameMs` (cv 3.13 %) is a better default than `meanFrameMs` on this scene.
- For sub-2 % work, keep using `mas-bunny` / `cube-wall-cloth` as the precision
  instrument and use `tumbler-garments` to show the win is not scene-specific.

### What this workload stresses that the official four do not

1. **Continuous collision detection over a dense self-contacting cloth pile.** The
   `InfoStacklessBVH*` family is the single largest cost centre at **30.9 %**, in absolute
   terms **7.3× `cube-wall-cloth`'s** whole BVH bill. Per launch, `filter_toi_k4` costs
   1426 µs here against 461 µs (cwc) and 300 µs (rwb) — it is *more work per launch*, not
   just more launches. Nothing in rounds 3–5 profiled a scene where CCD, not assembly,
   led the ranking.
2. **Shell bending as a first-order cost.** 18.4 % and the most expensive single kernel
   launch anywhere in the suite (3.48 ms). Normalised: **128 ns per hinge** here
   (27,158 hinges) and **145 ns per hinge** in `cube-wall-cloth` (12,160 hinges) — so the
   kernel is *intrinsically* slow per hinge and the tumbler merely has 2.2× more hinges
   and 3× more Newton iterations. A win here is measurable on `cube-wall-cloth`, whose
   scatter is 0.80 %.
3. **Contact assembly is relatively less dominant** (17.3 % vs 37.2 % in
   `rigid-wrecking-balls`), so the three rounds of `do_assemble_kernel` tuning have less
   left to give on the workload we actually care about.
4. **A very long, chaotic PCG tail.** 47,353 PCG iterations per run over 1,460 Newton
   iterations (32 per Newton) with a 14 % run-to-run spread, against `cube-wall-cloth`'s
   ~20,000 over ~510 (39 per Newton) at 1.5 %. The MAS preconditioner's inner kernels run
   52,294 times per run.

## Instrument validation (coordinator, after s00) — a null A/B measured a 4.94 % "win"

PERF_METHOD §2.7 says validate the instrument, not just the result. Before assigning any
optimization step, the coordinator ran the round's shared A/B harness
(`/workspace/output/round6/ab.py`) with **both arms set to the same env value on the same build**.
Anything it reports is by construction false.

| null A/B | ordering | meanFrameMs | medianFrameMs | ms/Newton-it | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tumbler-garments`, n=5 | ABAB, no warm-up | **−4.94 %** (p=0.23) | **−7.26 %** | **−5.52 %** (p=0.22) | false effect, arm A got the cold first run (174.9 ms against its own 147–163 ms) |
| `tumbler-garments`, n=5 | **ABBA + 1 discarded warm-up** | **−1.02 %** (p=0.82) | −4.28 % | **−1.31 %** (p=0.75) | the artefact is gone; what is left is the scene's own scatter |
| `cube-wall-cloth`, n=5 | ABBA + warm-up | +1.50 % (p=0.17) | +0.56 % | **+0.54 %** (p=0.19) | the precision instrument of this round |

Three things follow, and they govern every later step:

1. **A cold first run is worth ~5 % on this scene, and under ABAB ordering it lands entirely in the
   first arm.** The harness now runs one **discarded warm-up per arm** and orders the reps
   **ABBA**, so a monotone drift cancels instead of accumulating. Both are defaults; a step that
   overrides them must say so.
2. **The tumbler cannot gate a small effect.** Its own null moves ±1 % with single runs spanning
   134–169 ms. Treat it as the *ranking and confirmation* instrument: claim an end-to-end tumbler
   win only above ~4 %, always alongside the per-kernel scope gate. Sub-2 % work is gated on
   `cube-wall-cloth` (null ±0.54 % on ms/Newton-it) or `mas-bunny` (deterministic, Newton 465).
3. **Correction to s00's guidance: prefer the mean, not the median, on the tumbler.** s00 recommended
   `medianFrameMs` as the tighter statistic; under a null it is the *loosest* of the three
   (−7.26 % and −4.28 % against the mean's −4.94 % and −1.02 %). `ms_per_newton` is tightest, and
   only while Newton counts hold still.

The statistics themselves came through the test: every null was reported as overlapping with
p ≥ 0.17, and the iteration-count guard fired on the runs where PCG had moved. No number in this
round is trusted because the harness printed it — it is trusted when the scope gate and the
end-to-end gate agree, on a scene whose null envelope is smaller than the claimed effect.

**The premise checked first (PERF_METHOD §2.10/§2.11).** Round 5's s30 won 21 % on the sibling
`StableNeoHookean3D` G/H kernel with `__launch_bounds__(128, 3)`, and nothing in rounds 3–5 ever
put a bound on the hinge kernel, which is the more extreme case: 255 registers, 3 104 B of stack
frame, 1 168 B of spill traffic, **8 of the SM's 32 warp slots**. Round 4 had already written that
this kernel "is *not* at an FP64 roof". The hypothesis was therefore the §4 pattern — a lever built
in an earlier round that was never wired into the kernel it fits best. It is wrong, and the way it
is wrong is the finding.

**The old path is provably untouched.** A first attempt moved the body into a shared
`__device__ __forceinline__` function (s30's shape). Under `-rdc=true` that is **not** neutral: it
moved the unbounded kernel from 3 104 B of frame / 392 B cmem[2] to 3 616 / 440 — i.e. it would have
made arm A of its own A/B slower than the tree it was being compared against. The shipped form puts
the parameter list and the body in macros instead, so every wrapper expands to the same token stream
and all six pre-existing instantiations keep main's exact figures (6688 / 3472 / 6976 / 3840 / 3104 /
3328 B, same cmem). **Check this class of thing on the binary, not in the source.**

**What the bound actually does.** sm_75: 40 SMs, 65 536 registers and 32 warp slots each. 255
registers/thread admits 8 warps per SM *whatever the block size*, so the s20/s24 launch ramp — which
moves warps between blocks — cannot change occupancy at all. 168 registers admits 12.

| scene | grid x block | blocks/SM at 255 reg | rounds | blocks/SM at 168 | rounds | measured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cube-wall-cloth | 380 x 32 | 8 (320 resident) | **2** | 12 (480 resident) | **1** | **1.7555 -> 1.6656 ms/launch, −5.12 %** |
| tumbler-garments | 425 x 64 | 4 (160 resident) | **3** | 6 (240 resident) | **2** | **3.4488 -> 3.8728 ms/launch, +12.29 %** |

cube-wall buys a whole round and wins 5 %. The tumbler's rounds x warps product is unchanged
(3x8 = 2x12), so no round is saved and the bound delivers nothing but **+4.8 KB per thread of extra
spill traffic** (1 168/1 168 -> 3 472/3 680 B), which costs 12.3 %. Both distributions are disjoint
(5+4 runs on cube-wall, 3+3 on the tumbler, full runs, identical launch geometry in every arm).
`(128, 4)` — 128 registers, 16 warps — is worse on both: +2.1 % and +43.9 %.

**Diagnosis, stated so a later step does not retry it.** +50 % resident warps made the kernel
**12.3 % slower** on a scene where no round was saved. This kernel is **not latency-bound**; s30's
lever does not apply to it. Its local-memory traffic is what is expensive — +4.8 KB/thread of spill
costs roughly +70 % of per-warp time. PERF_METHOD §6 says occupancy tuning "often does not transfer
— it can flip sign"; here it flips sign **between two scenes on one GPU**, which is a stronger
statement than the round-5 cross-architecture one.

**End-to-end, for the record.** Both gates are below their scenes' resolution and the harness said
so rather than being talked into a number. cube-wall-cloth n=5: `ms_per_newton` −1.42 % (t=2.95,
p=0.019) — but PCG moved −1.77 % against a −1.77 % wall change and `ms_per_pcg` is +0.01 %, so the
iteration-count guard fired and the wall number is a trajectory shift, not throughput (the scope
gate predicts −0.6 %: the kernel is 12.5 % of cube-wall GPU time). tumbler-garments n=5: +3.89 %
mean, +5.30 % per Newton, overlapping (p=0.39 / 0.17), guard fired again — the scene cannot resolve
the predicted +2 %, but both statistics carry the scope gate's sign. **The scope gate is the verdict
here; the end-to-end gates only fail to contradict it.**

**Numerics.** The bounded wrappers are bit-identical to the unbounded one. Proof
(`ptx_identity.txt`, not an argument): the TU's PTX contains all three entry points; after erasing
the mangled name, the `.maxntid`/`.minnctapersm` directives (which *are* `__launch_bounds__`), the
per-function basic-block and local-depot numbering, the bodies differ in exactly two integer
instructions — `add.s32 %r,3` strength-reduced to `or.b32 %r,3`, and two independent `setp.gt.s32`
commuted into a commutative `and.pred`. The **FP64 instruction stream is identical, all 5 144
instructions in the same order**, so contraction and rounding are fixed before ptxas runs; ptxas then
only reallocates registers. Cross-check: `uipc_test_backend_cuda` and `uipc_test_sim_case` pass with
identical assertion counts under `UIPC_DSB_OCC=1`.

## s02 — the hinge PSD projection did not need to exist: a Gauss-Newton bending Hessian

Evidence: `agent_docs/performance/data/2026-09-13-round6-s02/`
(`static_resources.txt`, `sass_identity.txt`, `gradient_bit_identity.txt`,
`gn_math_probe.{cpp,txt}`, `kernel_scope.txt`, `launch_geometry.txt`,
`scene_executes_kernel.txt`, `convergence.txt`, `verify_audit_ab.txt`,
`gate.txt`, `gate_gn0.txt`, `ab/`, `nsys/`).

**The premise, checked against the code first (PERF_METHOD §2.11).** s01 proposed
this and it survives: `sym/discrete_shell_bending.inl` really does generate
`E = L0*kappa*(theta-theta_bar)^2/h_bar` and `ddEddtheta = 2*L0*kappa/h_bar` —
a constant that does **not** depend on `theta`, so `theta_bar != 0` changes
nothing. It is non-negative for every reachable input: `L0` is a rest edge
length, `h_bar = A/(3*L0)` a rest height, and `kappa` a bending stiffness
(`E*h^3/(12(1-nu^2))`). `discrete_shell_bending_function.h::ddEddx` assembles
exactly `dthetadx * ddEddtheta * dthetadx^T + dEdtheta * ddthetaddx`, so the
first term is a PSD rank-1 outer product of `grad(theta)` (**not** of the energy
gradient — `grad(E) = E' * grad(theta)`, so the brief's `g g^T` is the dihedral
gradient, not `dEdx`) and all of the indefiniteness is in `E' * hess(theta)`.

Measured confirmation over 200 000 randomised hinges against the real
`UIPC_GENERIC` functions (`gn_math_probe.cpp`, three deformation amplitudes):

| check | result |
|---|---|
| `max abs((H_full − H_gn) − E'*hess(theta)) / max abs(H_full)` | **3.8e-16** — the split is exactly what the premise says |
| `max abs(H_gn − H_gn^T)` | **0** — exactly symmetric (the assembler writes the four diagonal 3x3 blocks in full, so this matters) |
| `min eig(H_gn) / max abs(eig(H_gn))` | **−7.1e-16**, 0 / 200 000 samples with a meaningfully negative eigenvalue |
| rank of the **PSD-projected exact** Hessian | **4.00** of 9, every sample |
| `norm(H_full − H_gn)/norm(H_full)` | 0.059 at `mean abs(theta−theta_bar)` = 0.08 rad, 0.242 at 0.38 rad, 0.672 at 1.59 rad |

The rank line is the one worth keeping: **the exact path is not "the true
Hessian" either** — its PSD projection throws away five of nine directions at
every hinge. Gauss-Newton keeps one. The global system is still positive
definite through mass, membrane and contact.

**What shipped.** `Proj = 3` in the existing
`DiscreteShellBending_do_compute_gradient_hessian_kernel` template (a template
parameter, not a runtime flag, so neither instantiation pays for the other), and
`ddEddx_gauss_newton` beside `ddEddx`. `Vdt2` is folded into the rank-1
coefficient instead of scaling the assembled matrix (12 + 78 multiplies instead
of 12 + 144 + 144), and the fill is an explicit mirrored loop so the result is
*exactly* symmetric. `UIPC_DSB_GAUSS_NEWTON=0` restores the exact Hessian
followed by the s14/s19 projection, after which `UIPC_DSB_REDUCED_SPD`,
`UIPC_DSB_BLOCKED_PROJ` and `UIPC_MAKE_SPD_JACOBI` select it as before.
The Gauss-Newton path never calls `dihedral_angle_hessian` at all — that is why
the win is larger than s01's 80.5 % projection share.

## s03 — the contact barrier's PSD projection has a closed form, and three rounds have been optimising an eigen-solve around it

The brief asked whether s02's Gauss-Newton argument generalises to contact. It does,
**and more than the brief expected in one direction and less in another.**

*The kernel first.* `do_assemble_kernel` is not one kernel — **six** source files define a

*The friction family is already finished.* `friction_make_spd` collapses the 12x12 / 9x9 /
6x6 frictional Hessian to a **2x2 closed form** (`J J^T = ||w||^2 I_2`, so clamping commutes
with the scaled isometry), and two of the three branches of `friction_hessian` do not project
at all. There is nothing for this step to do there, and the brief's "friction may already be
handled" is correct.

### The algebra, and the correction that makes it work

`PT_/EE_/PE_/PP_barrier_gradient_hessian` literally assemble

    H = B''(D) gradD gradD^T + B'(D) hessD

with `D` the **squared** distance. Two measured facts (`gn_contact_probe.cu`, 200 000
randomised samples per pair type, run **on the device against the real `__device__`
functions** — they are `__device__`-only, so s02's host probe shape is not available):

1. **`B'' >= 0` and `B' <= 0` over the whole active domain and beyond it**, for both the
   plain and the thickness-shifted Stiff-GIPC log² barrier: `min B''/kappa = 9.3e-07`
   over 180 000 active samples and `1.1e-06` over 20 000 deliberately inactive ones,
   **0 negative**. Structurally: `B'' = 2kL² + 4k(2D−2d̂²)L/D − 2k(D−d̂²)²L/D² + 2k(D−d̂²)²/D²`
   with `L = log(D/d̂²) < 0` and `D − d̂² < 0` on the active domain, so **each of the four
   terms is non-negative there**. The first term of `H` is therefore PSD and all of the
   indefiniteness is in `B' hessD` — s02's argument, verbatim.
2. **The term that gets dropped is not small.** `max |B'| / (B'' D) = 0.9651`. Dropping it
   outright — the plain Gauss-Newton Hessian `c = B''` — **over-stiffens contact by a factor
   1.74** (`mean lambda_max(H_gn)/lambda_max(H_proj) = 1.7394 / 1.7412 / 1.7411 / 1.7418` for
   PT / EE / PE / PP) and leaves `||H_proj − H_gn||/||H_proj|| = 0.74`. Against s02's hinge,
   where the same ratio was 0.06–0.24, this is a far coarser approximation, and it measures
   like one (below).

**The correction costs one FMA.** Because `D` is a squared distance, `ghat^T hessD ghat =
|gradD|²/(2D)` — exactly for PP, to leading order for PE/PT/EE — so the rank-1 matrix

    H_spd ~= c gradD gradD^T,   c = B''(D) + B'(D) / (2 D)

reproduces the projected Hessian's leading eigenvalue exactly. `c >= 0` follows from
fact 2: `2 B'' D + B' >= (2 − 0.9651) B'' D > 0` (it is clamped anyway).

**Measured against the shipped projection, per pair type:**

| | PT | EE (un-mollified) | PE | PP |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `(H_exact − H_rank1) − B' hessD`, relative | 5.3e-07 | 2.7e-13 | 2.0e-13 | 3.5e-16 |
| exact `H` indefinite | 100 % | 100 % | 100 % | 100 % |
| **rank the shipped projection keeps** | **3 of 12** | **3 of 12** | **2 of 9** | **1 of 6** |
| `‖H_proj − c gg^T‖/‖H_proj‖`, mean / max | 1.8e-4 / 1.6e-1 | 2.7e-4 / 4.3e-1 | 9.8e-5 / 1.9e-2 | **4.8e-16 / 1.5e-15** |
| `lambda_max` ratio, mean / min | 1.000000 / 0.99595 | 1.000000 / 0.99577 | 1.000000 / 0.99981 | **1.000000 / 1.000000** |
| `H_rank1` exactly symmetric, never indefinite | yes, 0 / 200 000 | yes | yes | yes |

**This is the finding of the step.** The PSD eigen-solve that K10 (round 3), s16/s17
(round 4) and s25/s31 (round 5) have been making progressively cheaper produces, to four or
five digits, a matrix with a **closed form** — and for point-point it produces that matrix
*exactly*, to 1.5e-15. s02 found the hinge's projection was keeping rank 4 of 9; the contact
projection keeps 3 of 12, 2 of 9 and 1 of 6, and the leading direction carries 99.98 % of it.

### Why the tumbler's traversals are 3–5x more expensive per launch: it is pair count, and here is the number

The brief asked whether it is pair count, tree quality, divergence or atomics. A new device-side
probe (`UIPC_CCD_STATS=1`, four counters per pair type accumulated inside the ACCD loop) answers it
directly, over the first 400 `filter_toi` launches of a tumbler run:

| | EE | PT |
|---|---:|---:|
| ACCD calls | 275 236 843 | 96 673 289 |
| **candidate pairs per launch** | **688 092** | **241 683** |
| ACCD loop passes per call | **1.035** | 1.036 |
| calls that converge onto a hit | 1 040 933 (**0.378 %**) | 371 542 (0.384 %) |

**688 000 edge-edge candidates per Newton iteration**, and 99.6 % of them cannot collide within the
step. That is the whole explanation of the per-launch cost: it is not tree quality and not
divergence inside a long traversal — the ACCD loop runs essentially **once** per pair
(1.035 passes), so every candidate costs the same fixed amount and the launch cost is linear in
the candidate count. A dense self-contacting cloth pile simply has an order of magnitude more
swept-AABB-overlapping pairs than a cube on a wall.

### The dead work, and why removing it is exact rather than conservative-with-an-argument

`distance::edge_edge_ccd` (and its PT / PE / PP siblings) is the Codim-IPC **additive CCD**
loop. One pass is:

```
if(--max_iter < 0) return true;
tocLowerBound = (1 - eta) * dFunc / ((dist_cur + thickness) * maxDispMag);
advance ea0..eb1 by tocLowerBound          // 12 FMAs
flag = edge_edge_distance_flag(...);       // the expensive part
edge_edge_distance2(flag, ...);            // ... and the rest of it
if(toc && dFunc/(dist_cur + thickness) < gap) break;      // -> hit
toc += tocLowerBound;
if(toc > toc_prev) return false;                          // -> no hit in this step
```

`toc` is set to 0 immediately before the loop, so **on the first pass the `if(toc && ...)` break is
disabled by its own guard** and the only reachable exit of that pass is `toc > toc_prev`. When
`tocLowerBound > toc_prev` already holds, the advance and the full distance re-evaluation between
the two are dead: nothing reads their results before the function returns `false` with
`toc = 0 + tocLowerBound`. Testing it where `tocLowerBound` is computed returns the same `false`
with the same `toc` — `0 + x == x` exactly in IEEE — so this is an **exact transformation of the
shipped path, not a looser filter**. It cannot drop a pair the old path would have kept, and it
cannot report a larger time of impact.

That is the entire change: `if constexpr(EarlyOut) { if(toc == T(0) && tocLowerBound > toc_prev) { toc = tocLowerBound; return false; } }`,
in all four `*_ccd` functions, behind a new `template <typename T, bool EarlyOut = false, bool Stats = false>`.
The four `filter_toi_k*` kernels of the default `info_stackless_bvh` filter are templated on the
same two flags and dispatched from `UIPC_CCD_EARLY_OUT` (default 1) and `UIPC_CCD_STATS` (default 0),
so **neither is a branch or an extra register inside the shipped kernel**. Every other caller of
these functions keeps the defaulted `EarlyOut = false` and is untouched.

With the switch on, the same probe measures **99.2–99.5 % of ACCD calls taking the early exit** —
i.e. the dead work is removed from essentially the whole candidate list.

### Numerics: bit-identical, proved on the device against the real functions

`ccd_earlyout_probe.cu` runs both instantiations of all four `*_ccd` functions **in the same
thread on the same inputs**, over 10^6 randomised samples per pair type — primitives 0–20 mm
apart in a 20 mm box, displacement magnitudes log-uniform over 10^-4 to 1 m, `eta` uniform on
[0.1, 0.9], thickness 0 or up to 1 mm, and one sample in four deliberately near-parallel
(EE / PE) or near-degenerate (PT):

| | EE | PT | PE | PP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| samples | 1 000 000 | 1 000 000 | 1 000 000 | 1 000 000 |
| return-value mismatches | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| `toc` **bit** mismatches | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| a **larger** `toc` on the new path | **0** | **0** | **0** | **0** |
| hits on the old path (sanity: the samples do collide) | 19.47 % | 15.41 % | 11.08 % | 7.26 % |

This is the **bit-identical** level of PERF_METHOD §2.3, not the rounding level: the change does
not touch any arithmetic, it removes a computation whose result is not read. The third row is the
safety-critical one the brief asked for — the new path never reports a time of impact later than
the old one, on any sample, so it cannot let the solver take a step the old filter would have
refused.

### Transfer prediction

**Algorithmic — it transfers.** The step deletes, for 99.4 % of the candidate list, one
`*_distance_flag` + `*_distance2` evaluation and twelve FMAs per pair, at an unchanged launch
geometry, an unchanged candidate set and an unchanged output. Nothing about it depends on
occupancy, register pressure, spill traffic or wave quantisation — the categories PERF_METHOD §6
warns do not carry.

Two caveats a rented run should test:
- the removed work is FP64-heavy (a sqrt, several divides, the flag's sign tests), and this box
  runs FP64 at 1/32 rate. The *fraction* of the kernel that disappears will be smaller on a part
  with better FP64 throughput, so **−25 to −33 % per launch is an upper estimate**.
- the *scene share* of `filter_toi` will move between architectures the way every share does
  (§6): it is 13.4 % of the tumbler, 5.8–6.3 % of the three official contact scenes and 1.3 % of
  `mas-bunny` **on cc 7.5**.
- the **first-pass exit rate is a property of the workload, not the hardware**: 99.2–99.5 % on the
  tumbler, and the per-launch deltas on four very different scenes agree to within 8 points, so
  the mechanism itself should reproduce anywhere.

### The rule, fixed before the measurement

`VERDICT_RULE.md` was committed at `ab5adcf6`, before the first arm executed. Three arms on one
build at `7316bbc5`, `UIPC_CONTACT_RANK1` unset in all of them:

| arm | what it is | n |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | exact path (`UIPC_DSB_GAUSS_NEWTON=0`), default scene | 10 |
| **A'** | exact path, **perturbed initial state** — five distinct physically meaningless perturbations (±1e-9 m on one vertex, ±1e-6 rad on one garment's yaw, on four different garments) | 10 |
| **B** | Gauss-Newton (the shipped default) | 10 |

**R** (the reference distribution) = A ∪ A' pooled, n = 20: twenty runs that are *by construction*
the same physics, the same solver and the same code path, differing only by a perturbation between
1e-9 m and 3e-7 m — 1e-6 to 1e-4 of `d_hat` (1.27 mm) and of the element edge (6.2 mm).
**T** = B, n = 10. The rule: B fails if any safety item fails in B and not in R; or if an observable's
median leaves R's range; or if a two-sided Mann-Whitney has p < 0.05 **and** the median moves more
than half of R's own range (both required — with ~30 observables, p < 0.05 alone happens by chance);
or if the per-frame trajectory-divergence curve for A-vs-B leaves the envelope of the
physically-equivalent pairs. A widened spread alone was declared *not* a bias in advance, because
chaotic divergence widens spread; the bias signature is a one-directional median shift.

The pass started at n=5/arm. Three observables were marginal there, so n was raised to 10 —
the rule's own instruction — rather than the conclusion being rounded. **At n=5 the rule flagged
`pairs_last_quarter` as SYSTEMATIC; at n=10 it does not.** Both tables are in the evidence.

### Why arm A is itself a perturbation experiment (a correction to s00)

s00 recorded "frames 1-3 are bit-reproducible". That is true of the *iteration counts* and false of
the positions: two identical runs of the **un-edited** scene already differ by 1.4e-8 m in `r_max` by
frame 3. The scene has no RNG; the seed is GPU reduction order. So within-A scatter is already a
perturbation experiment with a ~1 ulp seed, and R spans the seed range 1e-16 … 3e-7 m. That is what
makes the envelope test meaningful.

### The +3 % Newton cost is the honest correction to s02

s02 reported Newton +0.70 % on the tumbler from n=5 wall-clock A/B runs and called the counts flat.
At n=10 on the physical-audit runs it is **+3.2 % (p=0.010)**, and it replicates at the tighter
tolerance (+4.2 %). PCG is +5.5 % / +4.0 %, line-search +2.6 %. None of these is anywhere near
s02's −18.6 % wall win, so the win stands; but "the Gauss-Newton search direction costs nothing in
Newton work" was too strong. It costs a few per cent on this scene. On the crease micro-test below it
*saves* iterations, so this is scene-dependent, not a general property.

Three things are worth reading off it.

1. **The Gauss-Newton Hessian is a 183-nanometre perturbation of the initial step.** That is 1.4e-4
   of `d_hat` and 3e-5 of an element edge — between the deliberate A' seed (4.0e-8 m) and the
   deliberate A'' seed (5.2e-7 m), and 158x the seed the GPU's own reduction order supplies for free.
2. **The growth rate is the same.** Frames 3→8, e-folding per frame: EQUIV 2.50, CROSS 2.01. The

   180-frame run**. The scene's divergence saturates by frame ~45 and stays saturated; a one-frame
   head start into an exponential that saturates 40 frames later is not observable in anything.
3. **The saturation level is the same.** 0.2165 m for physically equivalent pairs, 0.2303 m for
   exact-vs-Gauss-Newton, 0.239 m for two Gauss-Newton runs against each other. Two runs of the
   *exact path* that differ only by GPU reduction order end up **0.21 m apart** on a 0.30 m drum.
   That is the scale of this system's own sensitivity, and it is the number that makes per-frame
   agreement an unobtainable bar.

at every frame. With A, A' and A'' it does at **179 of 181 frames**. It sits above the envelope at
frames 4 and 5 by factors of 1.09 and 1.25 — the frames where the larger seed is still entering the
exponential — and is inside it from frame 6 on. **A'' was added precisely because the first pass'
A' seeds (1e-9 m) were smaller than B's, which put frames 1-5 outside for a reason that was seed size
and nothing else; widening the control to a seed larger than B's removed three of the five.** A''‘s
own observables all sit inside R (`app_check.txt`), including a `pairs_converged_last_quarter` of
17 542 — *below* every A and A' run and below eight of the ten B runs.

### The 2.15° drum transient, explained mechanically — and it is an *exact-path* event

`events_n10.txt`. Over 30 full runs, counting frames after the frame-1 start-up transient (which is
the drum accelerating from rest and is exactly 0.0396° in every run of every arm):

| arm | frames with \|err\| > 0.1° | runs with a > 1° event | max \|err\| after frame 1, median [min, max] |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (exact) | 1 of 1790 | 0 | 0.0042° [0.0014, **0.1455**] |
| A' (perturbed exact) | 4 of 1790 | **1** | 0.0061° [0.0026, **1.0072**] |
| **B (Gauss-Newton)** | **0 of 1790** | **0** | 0.0057° [0.0017, **0.0265**] |

**Every transient above 0.3° in this pass happened on the exact path, and none happened in ten
Gauss-Newton runs.** s02's 2.15° was one draw from a rare-event distribution that belongs to the
scene, not to the Hessian; the pre-registered one-sided test for `drum_track_err_deg_max` getting
*worse* in B returns p = 1.0.

The mechanism, read off the two > 0.3° frames (Ap_3 frame 94, −1.0072°; Ap_10 frame 56, −0.8704°) —
both show the identical signature:

1. a **contact-pair spike** lands inside the Newton loop: Ap_3 f94 iteration 3 jumps to
   PT 1120 / EE 4114 / PE 6408 / PP 1518 from 704 / 2558 / 2729 / 451 the iteration before;
   Ap_10 f56 iteration 2 jumps to 1245 / 5520 / 9851 / 3255 from 192 / 716 / 727 / 225;
2. the pile momentarily locks up and the **Newton increment collapses**:
   `MaxTranslationChecker Residual/AbsTol = 1.32e-4 / 8.33e-4` and `1.51e-4 / 8.33e-4`, i.e. 5-6x
   *below* tolerance, while `ABDToleranceChecker` also reports converged;
3. both checkers converged ⇒ `Newton Iteration Converged with Iteration Count: 3` (and `2`), against
   those runs' own means of 7.6 and 8.4;
4. the frame therefore ends with the drum's **soft** transform constraint (`SoftTransformConstraint`,
   strength 1e2) not yet pulled onto its commanded absolute pose — a one-frame lag of ~1°, about a
   quarter of the 4°/frame the drum turns at 40 rpm;
5. the next frame recovers (Ap_3 f95: −0.0175°, f100: −0.0002°). **`drum_turn_deg` is 720.000 of
   720.0 commanded in every run of every arm**, and `drum_track_err_deg_final` is ~3e-4°.

There is **no CCD clamp, no line-search cut and no CFL clamp** in either event
(`last_ccd_toi = last_line_search_alpha = last_cfl_alpha = 1.0`), which rules out the three
mechanisms the brief listed as candidates. It is the *increment-based* convergence test plus a soft
drive: pooled over all 30 runs, frames with |err| < 0.01° take a mean of 8.23 Newton iterations and
frames with |err| > 0.3° take **3.50**.

This is a property of s00's scene design — an increment test that cannot distinguish "converged"
from "momentarily stuck", and a drum driven softly rather than kinematically — and it is worth
recording as a scene limitation for whoever uses the tumbler next. It is not s02's.

### The crease-severity micro-test — the part that generalises beyond our five scenes

`crease_micro.py`, `crease_base.txt`, `crease_sharp.txt`. The dropped term is `E'(θ)·∇²θ` with
`E'(θ) = 2·L0·κ·(θ − θ̄)/h̄` — **linear in the crease angle**, against a kept term that is constant.
s02 measured `‖H_full − H_gn‖/‖H_full‖` at 0.059 / 0.242 / 0.672 for mean `|θ − θ̄|` of 0.08 / 0.38 /
1.59 rad. None of the five benchmark scenes goes near the top of that range, so a case was built that
does.

A flat cloth strip (rest angle `θ̄ = 0` at every hinge), **gravity off and contact disabled**, so the
hinge energy is the only thing that can make the solve hard. The two end rows are driven by a soft
position constraint onto the rigid rotation of their rest positions by ±φ about the fold axis, φ
ramping linearly to 145° over 200 frames; the strip is short along the fold direction and wide across
it, so the imposed turn is carried by a handful of hinge rows and the per-hinge angle gets large.
Severity is **measured, not assumed**: the dihedral angles are recomputed from the retrieved positions
every frame. Two configurations, 8 and 4 runs per arm. The test is near-deterministic — arm A totals
822 Newton iterations in all eight base runs, arm B 778-779 — so these are not noise.

projection keeps 4 of 9 directions and its output is *not* the true Hessian either, while
Gauss-Newton's rank-1 matrix is PSD by construction. Dropping an indefinite term makes the model
Hessian more conservative — a shorter, more robust step — not a worse one. **Within reachable
configurations, up to a complete fold, there is no regime where the Gauss-Newton hinge Hessian
degrades convergence.**

### Verdict: physics unchanged

No safety item fails in B that does not also occur in A or A'. No physical observable's median leaves
the reference distribution's range. No observable meets the pre-registered systematic-shift condition.
The trajectory-divergence envelope contains the exact-vs-Gauss-Newton curve at 179 of 181 frames and
at saturation, and the two frames it does not are accounted for, quantitatively, by seed size. The one
coherent shift the first pass found does not replicate. The two statistics s02 flagged are both
extreme-value artefacts of n=3, and the more dramatic of them — the drum transient — happens **only on
the exact path** in 30 runs, with a mechanism that is the scene's increment-based convergence test
plus a soft drum drive.

**s02's Gauss-Newton hinge Hessian should stay default-on.** The `UIPC_DSB_GAUSS_NEWTON=0` rollback is
proved to work at kernel level and should stay.

Two honest corrections to the round record come out of this pass and are worth more than the verdict:

1. **The Gauss-Newton direction costs +3 to +4 % Newton and +4 to +5 % PCG on the tumbler**, measured
   at n=10 and replicated, not the "flat" of s02's n=5 wall A/B. Against −18.6 % of wall time it does
   not change the decision, and on the crease micro-test it *saves* iterations — but it is not free.
2. **s00's "frames 1-3 are bit-reproducible" is about iteration counts, not positions.** Two identical
   runs of the un-edited scene differ by 1.4e-8 m in `r_max` by frame 3 and end **0.21 m apart** on a
   0.30 m drum. Any future claim about this scene's trajectory has to be made against that envelope.

### Limits, stated plainly

- **N runs is N runs.** 10 per arm on the main comparison, 10 per arm on the replication, 5 on A''.
  A real effect smaller than about a third of the null range would not be visible here; the null range
  on the pile-compaction statistics is a **factor of two**, which is why this scene cannot answer a
  small question about them at any affordable n.
- **One scene.** The three-arm experiment is `tumbler-garments` only. `cube-wall-cloth` and
  `stiff-gipc-case2` also run the hinge kernel and were *not* given the same treatment; their s02
  observables (`cloth_min_y`) are the only cross-scene evidence and they were n=5.
- **One GPU.** Everything is cc 7.5. The iteration-count difference is a property of the search
  direction and is architecture-independent; nothing else here is a throughput claim.
- **Short sanitizer runs.** 6 / 4 / 2 frames. Sanitized runs are 100-1000x slower, so these windows do
  not cover the dense-pile steady state that starts around frame 15, and `uipc_test_sim_case` was not
  run under any sanitizer.
- **The contact-pair probe counts the active set**, not contact forces, and excludes the broad-phase
  candidates and the vertex-half-plane pairs (which are 0 for this scene throughout).
- **`cc_min_dist` is a vertex-vertex proxy** for the true point-triangle gap, not the gap itself. It
  bounds it from above, it is the same proxy in every arm, and it never reached zero — but a genuine
  self-intersection test over 19 920 triangles per frame was not run.
- **The tolerance half of the replication is weaker than it looks**: `velocity_tol` binds on only ~11
  of 180 frames in this scene because the semi-implicit β rule terminates the rest. The replication
  reading stands; the "truncation shrinks with the tolerance" reading does not.
- **The crease micro-test has no contact and no gravity** — deliberately, to isolate the hinge — so it
  says nothing about how the Gauss-Newton direction interacts with a contact-dominated regime. It also
  reaches θ = π by construction, which is the end of the reachable range, not a sample of it.

### Found outside this step's area (reported, not fixed)

- **`tumbler-garments` does not stop Newton on `velocity_tol`.** `newton/semi_implicit/enable`
  defaults to **1**, and `SimEngine::convergence_check` returns on the accumulated-β rule
  (`beta_tol` 1e-3, `K_min` 6) *before* it ever calls the tolerance manager. Over a 180-frame run
  only **11 of 1 323** tolerance checks are all-converged; the other ~169 frames exit on β at 7-8
  iterations. Anybody tuning this scene's accuracy through `newton/velocity_tol` is turning a knob
  that binds on 6 % of its frames.
- **`MaxTranslationChecker` is an increment test, and a contact-pair spike can satisfy it.** It
  compares the *Newton increment* against `velocity_tol * dt`, so a frame in which the cloth pile
  momentarily locks up reports "converged" at iteration 2-3 with a residual 5x below tolerance while
  the soft-constrained drum is still ~1° behind its commanded pose. That is the whole mechanism of
  the tumbler's rare drum transient, and it is a property the scene's `SoftTransformConstraint` drive
  inherits. A kinematic drum, or a force-residual check alongside the increment check, would remove it.
- **`verify.py`'s `verify_ok` triangle-height threshold is 1.82 mm, not the 3.27 mm the narrative
  quotes.** The audit asserts `tri_height_min > 2*(r + d_hat)*0.4` = 1.82 mm; s00's `2r + d_hat` =
  3.27 mm is the *physical* "permanent self-contact" reference, and the two are easy to conflate.
  Both are stated separately above for that reason.
- **`UIPC_DSB_OCC` does not exist on main.** s01's ledger row says "kept in the branch default off",
  which reads like a shipped switch; `grep -rn UIPC_DSB_OCC src/` returns nothing on this head.
- **s00's garment centroid-travel figures are an n=1 sample and are not representative.** s00 records
  557 / 714 / 332 / 739 deg; at n=10 on the same code path the medians are 682 / 829 / 798 / 788 with
  per-garment run-to-run ranges as wide as 128-1030 deg. The qualitative statement ("carried and
  slipping") survives; the numbers should not be quoted as a reference.
- **Round 5's two sanitizer findings are still exactly where that pass left them**, at identical
  counts: the CUB `ExclusiveSum` uninitialised read from `GlobalDyTopoEffectManager::_distribute`,
  and the `MASPreconditionerEngine_*` racecheck warnings. Neither has an owner yet.
