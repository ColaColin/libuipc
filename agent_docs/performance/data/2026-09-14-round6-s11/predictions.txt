s11 predictions -- recorded BEFORE any s11 measurement was taken
================================================================

Context: s10 shipped UIPC_ABD_GH_PREPASS=1 (arm the fork before the contact
phase, enqueue the ABD body-local G/H after compute_dytopo_effect() returns) at
-5.19 % GPU busy union / -5.07 % ms per Newton on rigid-wrecking-balls. s10 also
measured that 0 % of the prepass lands inside contact part 1's window; its
412 ms of empty shadow is still empty, and the prepass hides under the
*post-contact* chain instead.

s11 adds two call sites inside the K9 contact fork:
  =3  between part 1's launch (side stream) and part 2's launch (default stream)
  =4  right after part 2's launch, still inside the fork

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
P1. Why mode 1 misses part 1's shadow -- a HOST reason, found in the code
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
GlobalDyTopoEffectManager::Impl::_distribute() does a *blocking* D2H copy
(`VarView<IndexT>{selected_hessian_offsets.data()+N}.copy_to(&h_total_count)`,
global_dytopo_effect_manager.cu:404) right after the scan, and _convert_matrix's
converter does the same kind of thing. So inside compute_dytopo_effect() the
host stalls until the contact kernels have drained. SimEngine's
launch_assembly_prepass() is only reached *after* that stall -- which is exactly
s10's "ortho starts 2 us after part 1 ends". This is a host run-ahead limit, not
a GPU scheduling one, and it is why moving the call site (not the stream) is the
whole of this step.

PREDICTION P1: with =3/=4 the ortho/kinetic prepass kernels WILL start before
contact part 1 ends (overlap fraction inside part 1's window > 0 %, expected
60-100 % of the prepass duration). This is the mechanism claim and it is the one
I expect to be confirmed most cleanly.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
P2. What the placement can and cannot buy -- stated before building it
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
It CANNOT buy much on top of s10, and the reason is arithmetic, not scheduling:

  s10 =0 busy union 3094.7 ms -> =1 busy union 2934.2 ms = -160.5 ms.
  The prepass itself is 158.8 ms of kernel time.

s10 already removed ~100 % of the prepass from the critical path. The prepass is
not partially hidden and waiting to be hidden better; it is *already fully
hidden*, just under a different shadow. Moving it into part 1's shadow therefore
does not add a second -5 %; it moves the same 158.8 ms from one pocket of idle
time to another.

PREDICTION P2 (the headline): =3 and =4 will land within +-1.5 % of =1 on rwb
ms/Newton -- i.e. between about -4 % and -6.5 % vs =0, NOT -8 % or better. The
gain REPLACES s10's, it does not stack on it. If a next step wants the 412 ms,
it has to bring *new* work to put in it (the FEM prepass, or a second ABD
stage), not re-place the same 158.8 ms.

P2b. The one way =3/=4 could beat =1 by more than noise: if the post-contact
chain that currently hides the prepass is itself *lengthened* by hosting it. s10
measured ortho at 332.2 us/launch in =1 vs 331.7 in =0 -- no contention cost --
so I expect this residual to be near zero on rwb. On cube-wall-cloth s10
measured ortho +11.6 % under contention, so cwc is where =3/=4 has the best
chance of beating =1, and the effect there is still bounded by cwc's total ortho
share.

P2c. The one way =3/=4 could LOSE to =1: part 2 is 91.7-98.3 % hidden inside
part 1 (s09). Adding a third resident kernel to part 1's leftover SMs can push
part 2 past part 1's end and put part 2 on the critical path for the first time.
Slot 3 (before part 2 is queued) is the more exposed one; slot 4 (after part 2
is queued) should protect part 2 by submission order, the same way s10's mode 1
protects part 1. PREDICTION: if the two slots differ, slot 4 >= slot 3.

P2d. Part 1's own per-launch time should be essentially unchanged (< +2 %) in
=3/=4, unlike mode 2's +567 us start delay, because part 1 is already queued
when the prepass is enqueued. If part 1 slows down measurably, the
submission-order model that s10 built is wrong and that is the finding.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
P3. The =2 instrument disagreement
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
s10: =2 reads -5.93 % ms/Newton end to end (n=8, disjoint, p=2.2e-07) and
+0.00 % on the nsys GPU busy union.

PREDICTION P3: the end-to-end instrument is the one telling the truth and nsys
is the perturbed one. The reason is P1: without a profiler the host reaches the
arm hook, records the fork, enqueues the prepass, and then *immediately stalls*
in _assemble's "Report Extent" D2H -- so on an un-instrumented run the prepass
gets a head start in a window when the GPU is otherwise draining the previous
iteration, and it is largely finished before part 1 is even issued. nsys adds
per-launch host overhead, which changes exactly that race.

I expect =2 to reproduce at -5 to -6.5 % vs =0 at n>=10 without a profiler, and
to be statistically indistinguishable from =1 (as it already was at n=8,
t=1.77, p=0.10). I do NOT expect =2 to beat =1 by a resolvable margin.

Note on the brief's suggested instrument: WB_TIMER=1 is NOT usable for this
question. Timer::set_sync_func installs cuda_tool::wait_device() and every
Timer scope entry/exit synchronises the whole device (sim_engine.cu:64), so the
timer tree destroys precisely the host run-ahead and the kernel overlap that the
three arms differ in. Under WB_TIMER all three arms are forced serial and would
read the same. The independent non-perturbing instrument is the un-instrumented
end-to-end benchmark at a sample size that can resolve the difference between
arms, which is what I run.

P4. Controls
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
mas-bunny and stiff-gipc-case2 launch zero ABD G/H kernels (s10's census), so
=3/=4 there is pure hook overhead plus one extra `find<GlobalLinearSystem>()` at
build time. Expect |delta| < 0.5 % and unresolved.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
P5. Correctness
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Bit-identical. The side stream's only ordering constraint is the fork event,
recorded at the same point in all of modes 1/2/3/4, so the GPU-visible
dependency graph is identical across them; only the host's enqueue point moves.
Expect UIPC_ABD_GH_PREPASS_VERIFY=1 to report 0 mismatching words for =3 and =4,
and gate.sh to match baseline_tests.txt counts.
